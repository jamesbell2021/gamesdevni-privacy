# Privacy Policy — Wee Man

*Last updated: 25 November 2026*

Wee Man is a game made by **3BL_Indie** (Northern Ireland).

## The short version

Wee Man has no accounts, no adverts and no analytics or tracking. Nothing is ever sent to us, and we do not
collect or store any information about you. His AI and his voice run on your own computer, so talking to him
does not send your words out over the internet.

If another PC on your home network is also running Wee Man, the two games talk to each other directly. That
exchange includes a short extract of the wee man's recent memories, and his memories include what you have
typed to him — see **Neighbours** below.

## His AI and his voice run on your PC

Wee Man's words are written by an AI model, and spoken by a voice program, that both run **on your own
computer** and are included inside the game. They use only your PC's own local address (127.0.0.1). Nothing you
type to him, and nothing he says, is sent over the internet, to us or to anyone else.

## What stays on your PC

All of this is kept in the game's own data folder on your PC, is never uploaded, and is deleted when you
uninstall the game:

- **Him** — his name, look, personality, mood and meters, and how long he has lived.
- **His memories**, including a record of what you have typed to him.
- **What he writes** — poems, stories, diary entries, dreams, songs, his Spectrum programs, his obituary.
- **Past Wee Men** — their ghosts, their obituaries, and the Book of Knowledge they hand down.
- **The house and your settings** — the fire, curtains, plants, the cat, subtitles, jump scares, window size.
- **Photographs** he takes at big moments. These are pictures of the game only, never of your screen or desktop.
- **When you play** — the hour you tend to start, how long you stay, which button you press first. He uses this
  to notice your habits and remark on them.
- On Windows the game also writes **log files** (in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\WeeMan`) to help diagnose faults.

The game also uses your **Documents** folder for two things you can see and manage yourself: it saves his
exported Spectrum programs to `Documents\Wee Man Programs`, and it reads ZX Spectrum tape files you put in
`Documents\Wee Man Tapes`, so that he can watch you play them.

## Neighbours (other PCs on your home network)

If another PC on the **same home network** is running Wee Man, the two games find each other with a small
announcement on the local network and then exchange messages directly, so the wee men can be neighbours: send
postcards and presents, swap tunes and invented words, visit at Christmas, telephone one another, and fall out
over the garden gnome. This never leaves your home network, and nothing goes through our servers (we do not have
any). It uses UDP port 5001 and TCP port 5002, so Windows may ask whether to let the game through its firewall.

The announcement your copy sends, roughly once a minute, contains his name, look, mood and meters, how long he
has been alive, whether it is his birthday, whether he is poorly, your PC's local clock time, an identifier for
the installation, and **a short extract of the three most recent things that happened to him** (about 90
characters each). That extract is taken from his memories, and **his memories include what you have typed to
him, so something you have typed may appear in it** and be seen by other copies of Wee Man on your network. A
neighbouring copy may also show that extract to its own wee man's AI, which likewise runs on that PC.

The messages he sends deliberately — postcards, what he says at a party, what he says on the telephone — are
written in his own voice by the AI on your PC. They are not copies of what you typed.

Anything arriving from a neighbour stays on your PC and is treated as untrusted: it is length-limited and
checked before the game uses it, and gifts are restricted to a fixed list of known items.

This is active by default, and at present there is no setting in the game to switch it off. It only does
anything while another copy of Wee Man is running on the same network at the same time. If you would rather it
did nothing, do not run Wee Man on a network where another copy is running, or block those two ports for the
game in your firewall.

## Links

The gold book on his bookshelf, and the thank-you screen at the end of the free demo, have a button that opens
our website in your web browser. If you press it, that website's own privacy policy applies. The game does not
open any other web address.

## Microsoft Store

If you got the game from the Microsoft Store, Microsoft handles the purchase and download under its own privacy
statement (<https://privacy.microsoft.com>). We receive only the anonymous sales and crash summaries that
Microsoft provides to every developer.

## Children

The game never asks for your name, your age, an email address or any other personal detail, and it sends nothing
to us. If children play it on a shared network, please read **Neighbours** above, because what is typed to the
wee man can be seen by other copies of the game on that same network.
